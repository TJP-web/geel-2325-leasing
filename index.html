The Building

Two buildings. Four floors and a rooftop.

23-25 West 35th Street is a ground-up building on West 35th between Fifth and Sixth Avenues: two adjoining structures, four floors, a cellar, and a rooftop, approximately 19,900 rentable square feet. To be delivered to shell, targeting mid-Q3 2026, with new gas and power in place. Each floor leases individually.

Ground floors are built for flagship food and beverage; upper floors for beauty, wellness, lifestyle, and fashion; the rooftop for a tea house or F&B amenity. The landlord delivers core kitchen infrastructure, so tenants build out faster and open sooner.

Why this building

A building made for retail, not the base of one.

Almost all Manhattan retail is the ground floor of an office or residential tower. This is a whole building, built from the ground up for retail and F&B, which changes what a tenant can do with it.

01

A whole building for retail

Purpose-built and vertical, not a tower base. Take one floor, several, or the entire building as a flagship with its own identity.

02

Turnkey F&B infrastructure

The landlord delivers new gas service, up to 400-amp electrical service per floor, and core kitchen infrastructure, so kitchens build out faster and for less.

03

Your own frontage

An individual lease with your own entrance, signage, and street presence on West 35th: brand visibility a tower base cannot offer.

04

A rooftop floor

A rooftop for F&B or hospitality: a rare, experiential level, on its own or bundled with the top floor.

Leasing structure

How the building leases.

One rule. Four ways it plays out.

Upper floors lease one tenant per floor, and are not subdividable. Only the ground floor splits, into Unit A and Unit B, each leasable on its own with its cellar. A tenant can take a single storefront or scale up to the whole building.

Strategy 01

Single Storefront

One ground unit and its cellar, on its own. The entry point: a flagship storefront on 35th with kitchen depth below. Unit A or Unit B.

Location

At the heart of three districts.

23-25 sits where three of Midtown's strongest commercial districts overlap: the Penn District, Herald Square and Macy's, and Koreatown. Fourteen subway lines run through Herald Square and Penn Station, both a short walk away, and the Empire State Building is minutes east.
